I build systems that sit at the intersection of generative AI and production engineering — models that don't just perform well in a notebook, but hold up at scale, in the loop, in front of real users.

My path here started in research. As a MASc candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of British Columbia, I worked on video diffusion models for echocardiogram analysis and data augmentation — you can read the [full thesis here](https://open.library.ubc.ca/soa/cIRcle/collections/ubctheses/24/items/1.0448491). I was fortunate to be co-supervised by [Prof. Renjie Liao](https://lrjconan.github.io/) and [Prof. Purang Abolmaesumi](https://ece.ubc.ca/purang-abolmaesumi/), whose rigor shaped how I approach every model I ship since.

Today I'm a Senior Machine Learning Scientist at Lily AI, where I design LLM-powered, agentic pipelines, sharpen model attribution, and take generative systems from prototype to production at scale. My focus sits squarely at the seam between research and engineering: diffusion models, applied generative AI, and the MLOps discipline that keeps them running.

That research-to-production throughline shows up in my publication record too — work published at ICCV, CVPR, and MICCAI — alongside recognition including the Canada Graduate Scholarship (CGS-M) and an NSERC USRA. What drives all of it is the same question: how do we turn state-of-the-art AI research into systems people can actually depend on?

Always up for a conversation on agentic AI, generative modeling, or ML systems at scale — reach out below.
